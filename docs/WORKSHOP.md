# Grid load, assumptions, and decisions — a 20-minute exercise

Audience: an energy systems group, applied-math club, or introductory class.
Prerequisites: percentages, maxima, and the difference between power and energy.
Materials: [Historical Lab](https://gert-d.vercel.app/history), a spreadsheet or
calculator, and the [source/reproduction guide](HISTORICAL_LAB.md).

## Session

1. **Minutes 0–3:** Ask participants to identify observations and assumptions in
   the summer case. Explain MW (power) versus MWh (energy).
2. **Minutes 3–7:** Set capacity to 80,000 MW. Compare 0%, 5%, and 10% reductions.
   Before each change, predict whether exceedance hours and gap energy increase,
   decrease, or remain unchanged. Explain the piecewise-linear response.
3. **Minutes 7–12:** Export the CSV and independently sum the positive gaps. For
   example, a one-hour load of 82,000 MW at 80,000 MW capacity gives 2,000 MWh
   before reduction. At 5% assumed reduction, adjusted load is 77,900 MW and the
   gap is zero. This arithmetic is illustrative, not a claim about an event.
4. **Minutes 12–16:** Compare spring and winter. Discuss why observed winter
   load during outages cannot establish unconstrained demand or outage losses.
5. **Minutes 16–20:** Each participant proposes one missing constraint and one
   change that would make the product clearer or more useful. Record the
   actual review, not a favorable rating requested by the facilitator.

## Evidence to retain after an actual session

- Date and host, with permission to identify the host publicly.
- Number who attended, number who completed a calculation, and number who
  submitted substantive feedback. Record separately; don't substitute signups.
- One exported report or independently reproduced result per completed task.
- Feedback, the resulting issue, the product change, and any retest.
- Whether a second session was requested or held; distinguish the two.

Attendance names, contact details, or student work should not be published
without permission. A local class can keep anonymous aggregate counts and
retain a host confirmation. Hosting this exercise is not institutional
endorsement, formal validation, or operational deployment of the software.
