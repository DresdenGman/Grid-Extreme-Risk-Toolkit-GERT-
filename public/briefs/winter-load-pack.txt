SUBJECT IDEA
Why can electricity use fall during a blackout?

READY-TO-FORWARD TEXT
Why can electricity use fall during a blackout? GERT offers a short,
source-linked exercise using 72 hourly observations from the February 2021
Texas winter storm. Explore the recorded load, distinguish what the curve
shows from what it cannot establish, and try a capacity assumption in the
Historical Lab. Includes a downloadable chart, original-source links, and a
self-check. Free to try, with no account required. This is a historical
educational exercise, not a live forecast or an estimate of prevented outages.

https://gert-d.vercel.app/briefs/winter-load

ALT TEXT
72 hourly ERCOT native-load observations, February 14–16, 2021, on a zero-to-
80-GW scale. A dashed line marks the reported onset of rotating outages at
01:25 CST on February 15. The curve falls but does not measure unmet demand
or reliability. All values and sources are available with the exercise.

INTERPRETATION AND PRIVACY
The event marker indicates onset, not the amount or duration of lost load.
The load curve does not identify how much electricity customers would have
used without interruptions. Do not label the drop as measured outage losses,
energy savings, improved reliability, or GERT's effect on the grid.
The two-question self-check runs locally in the visitor's browser. Its optional
download is self-reported, contains no name or email, and is not a certificate
or independently verified participant record. There is no automatic submission.
